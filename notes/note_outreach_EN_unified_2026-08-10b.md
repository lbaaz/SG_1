## Abstract

We study the self-interacting Pais–Uhlenbeck (PU) oscillator — the interaction inherited from the fourth-order equation of motion itself, x⁗ + (ω₁²+ω₂²)ẍ + ω₁²ω₂²x = g x³, and its degree-p generalizations — classically and quantum mechanically. **Classically, at the quartic degree**, we map the amplitude threshold s\*(ω₂/ω₁, g) separating Smilga-benign islands from finite-time blow-up, and find: an invariant K = g·s\*²; an empirical threshold law K = C·(ω₂−ω₁)²(ω₁+ω₂) with C = 0.272 ± 0.035 on the validity edges (C = 1/4 within errors) — equivalently a detuning-blind amplitude condition g·A₂\*²·(ω₁+ω₂) = 4C along our initial-condition family; a parity selection rule (only k+l even resonances act); a resonant normal form near 1:1 whose slow potential (3g/2)|κ₁ᾱ+κ₂β|⁴ possesses an exact cancellation valley — the escape channel, verified dynamically; and a finite threshold s\* = 0.70 at exact degeneracy (the quartic stabilizes the Jordan block), which explains a plateau K₀ = 0.0245 below ε_c ≈ 0.22. **Across degrees p ∈ {4, 5, 7}**, a pre-registered campaign then confronted the apparent affine law ln s\*_p = A(ω₂)·u_p + B(ω₂), u_p = 1/(p−2), with its own exact annihilator E(ω₂) = ln s\*₄ − 2.25·ln s\*₅ + 1.25·ln s\*₇: **refuted, 11/11 points, no fit, no lever**. The structured residual field E(ω₂) is then read by a derived parity/rank selection rule (independently FFT-controlled; verified out of sample 5/5 on archived sweeps), a measured saturation of the 3:1 channel at p = 4, and a resolved negative resonance canyon of E at the 5:2 core (interior minimum at ω₂ = 2.52); a dual-observable test at 8/3 was blocked by its own geometry — the exclusion zone around the site is itself degree-selective — and is consigned as non-concluding. **Quantum mechanically**, in the Hermitian indefinite realization H = −ω₁n₁ + ω₂n₂ + (g/4)x⁴, we work by exact diagonalization (up to 5184 states), and we lead with the spectral statement. A census of island-dominant eigenstates finds **no bound island eigenstate**, and the criterion is fixed by a positive control: in the bounded twin obtained by flipping the sign of the ghost mode, 74/74 island-dominant eigenstates are bound at machine precision, whereas in the ghost system none is, with a median twelve to thirteen orders of magnitude above the twin's floor — the metastability is structural, not preparation-dependent. Quantitatively, the time-averaged weight in a *fixed* occupation shell (35 ≤ max nᵢ ≤ 45, propagation-free) exceeds the twin's by 10⁴–10¹² for every island state tested, the separation growing with depth in the island. Dynamically, a short-time escape rate Γ_st — the initial slope of island survival, monotone in s — is truncation-stable for s ≳ 1.2 (Γ₆₄/Γ₇₂ = 0.95–1.10) and merely indicative below it; deep inside the island, where the rate's value is not converged, the existence of the leak rests not on the rate but on the no-ghost control (four to seven orders of magnitude above the bounded twin) and on the fixed-shell observable. Island survival falls and then plateaus, so 1/Γ_st is an initial escape time scale and not an asymptotic lifetime; the flux is shell-independent and robust to the regularization. At fixed classical point (g·s² const.), the ħ-dependence of the rate is a steep power law Γ ~ ħ⁴⁻⁵ — not the exponential e^(−S/ħ) of deep tunnelling — in our deep-quantum window. We position these results against Smilga's conjecture (SIGMA 5 (2009) 017), Gross–Strumia–Teresi–Zirilli (PRD 103, 115025), Damour–Smilga (2022), the recent discrete-spectrum theorem of Deffayet, Fathe Jalali, Held, Mukohyama and Vikman (arXiv:2604.21826), and a dedicated prior-art review of the classical maps (§6.2), and we ask three questions: is this known, is it sound, and is it useful to you?

## 1. Context: the questions this note addresses

The PU oscillator with a quartic interaction is the minimal arena for the fate of ghosts beyond the free theory. Its classical phenomenology — a benign "island of stability" around the origin, a shore, and an "infinite ocean of blow-up" — was identified by Smilga [1,2] and named in exactly these terms by Damour and Smilga [3], who scope their benignity conjecture to *globally* benign systems and leave the quantum dynamics of the island case "to future work". Smilga's own quantum statement for the interacting case is a self-labelled conjecture ("We do not know whether a mathematical theorem to this effect can be proven" [2]), argued through the −κ/r² analogy for low-lying states near the singularity; the fate of a quantum state *prepared on the island* is, to our knowledge, never posed in [1–3].

Gross, Strumia, Teresi and Zirilli (GSTZ) [4] introduced the concept we test: ghost metastability, "similar to vacuum decay", with a numerical ground-state computation in the toy model H = p₁²/2 − p₂²/2 + V + λq₁²q₂²/2 and the classical Birkhoff–Nekhoroshev framework. Their paper contains no rates and studies only the ground-like state; notably, they display the interaction actually inherited from the fourth-order oscillator — their Eq. (10), of type (q₁+q₂)⁴/Δ² — and then set it aside for the q₁²q₂² toy.

Most recently, Deffayet, Fathe Jalali, Held, Mukohyama and Vikman [5] proved that ghostly systems in a globally stable, Liouville-integrable class have **discrete** spectra — counterexamples to the expected spectral denseness. Their class has no islands: protection is global or absent.

On the classical side, a dedicated prior-art sweep (2004–2026; §6.2) found that two objects central to this note exist nowhere in the published literature: a stability/blow-up frontier of a ghost system traced against the frequency ratio ω₂/ω₁, and against the degree p of the interaction. The degree appears only as an analytic *sufficient condition* for stability (Held [13]; Deffayet et al. [14]); the ratio only as a qualitative distinction (dense/continuous spectrum [2]; the island "shrinks to a point" at equal frequencies [3]). A consensus is meanwhile emerging that classical benignity, where proven, traces back to hidden integrable structure (the integrable classes of [12], the taxonomy of [8], the theorem of [5]); the present campaign measures the opposite shore — the malicious side, chaotic resonant transport toward blow-up — which the review of §6.2 found unquantified in the literature.

**The gaps we address:** classically, the quantitative maps s\*(ω₂) and s\*(p, ω₂) and the law structure they carry; quantum mechanically, the fate of *island-protected* states in the *physical* PU-induced interaction. Our headline for the latter: global protection quantizes to discreteness (their theorem); insular protection quantizes to metastability (our measurement).

## 3. Classical results I — the quartic maps

**(a) Threshold invariant and law.** Across four systems and two couplings the g-exponent of s\* is −0.47…−0.50: K ≡ g·s\*² is the invariant (out-of-sample errors identical at both g). On the landscape edges (ω₂ ∈ [1.25, 1.5] ∪ [2.75, 3.25], ω₁ = 1) the law K = C·(ω₂−ω₁)²(ω₁+ω₂) holds at the 5–15% level, with C = 0.272 ± 0.035 over 17 edge points. C = 1/4 sits at +0.6σ, suggesting the closed form g·s\*² = (ω₂−ω₁)²(ω₁+ω₂)/4; 3/(4π) is at +0.9σ and not excluded. We have **not** derived C. **Equivalent, detuning-blind form (verified algebraically, then on the landscape data):** along our initial-condition family A₂ = −2s/Δ, so the law reads g·A₂\*²·(ω₁+ω₂) = 4C — the ε² factor was pure kinematics of the s→A map. If C = 1/4, the threshold is g·A₂\*² = 1/(ω₁+ω₂): a critical high-mode amplitude, blind to the detuning — which retroactively explains why one constant fits both landscape edges. A per-point spot check sharpens the picture: the right edge alone gives C = 0.269 ± 0.011 (n = 11; the stiffness-matched flagship (1, 2.85) lands at 0.996 of the 1/4 form; a mild ~10% drift across the window and the known first-cell bias remain to be resolved — 1/4 sits at +1.7σ statistical, 3/(4π) at ~2.7σ), while the left edge is resolution-limited at the current s-grid (±20–40% per point), superseding the blanket 5–15% accuracy quoted above. One structural caveat is thereby promoted to the central open question: all our classical and quantum measurements follow a single ray in the (A₁, A₂) plane (the ratio fixed by the initial-condition family); mapping the blow-up frontier in the full plane — isotropic in action (Chirikov overlap) versus aligned with the 1:1 cancellation combination (the valley mechanism) — is the announced discriminating test. Between the edges, a destabilized valley ω₂ ∈ [1.75, 2.6] (up to a factor ≈ 2 below the law) coincides with the overlap region of the 2:1 and 3:1 resonance neighbourhoods (Chirikov-type reading).

**(b) Parity selection rule.** The interaction is even in x, so only resonances with k+l even act at leading orders. Verified: no 3:2 feature (forbidden); weak 2:1 (only through (4,2) at second order); 3:1 present but single-channel.

**(c) The 3:1 channel is self-saturating.** The pair-creation channel A³B (zero cost at ω₂ = 3ω₁) produces no threshold suppression: stiffness-matched thresholds at ω₂ = 2.85 vs 3.00 are 8.10 vs 9.58 (and equal at larger g). This verifies, in the PU case, the general mechanism of GSTZ §2.4 (quartic action terms ~J² dominate the dangerous ~J^{3/2} terms at large J).

**(d) The 1:1 valley — where the physical interaction differs qualitatively from the toy.** Near degeneracy the resonant normal form of the PU quartic is
  V_slow = (3g/2) |κ₁ᾱ + κ₂β|⁴,  κᵢ² = 1/(2Δωᵢ),
with conserved M = J_A − J_B and an **exact cancellation valley** κ₁ᾱ = −κ₂β: both modes grow jointly in antiphase, x stays small, the interaction is blind, and the indefinite free energy costs nothing — the ghost escapes by becoming invisible. Verified dynamically: during a (1, 1.05) runaway, mode energies grow ×500 with E₁/E₂ → 1.01 and cancellation ratio ρ = max|x|/(amp₁+amp₂) → 0.002; a far-detuned runaway (1, 2.85) shows neither signature. By contrast, GSTZ's q₁²q₂² toy is *safe* at 1:1 (their resonant form is bounded, 1 + cos4Q/2 > 0). The interaction inherited from the actual fourth-order oscillator — their own Eq. (10) — is not: this is a counter-witness, within the physically motivated model, to "in most models, resonances do not lead to ghost run-away".

**(e) Exact degeneracy has a finite threshold.** At ω₂ = ω₁ (Jordan block, secular free theory) the interacting system is nevertheless bounded below s\*(ε=0) = 0.70: the quartic *stabilizes* the Jordan block at small amplitude. K(ε) therefore plateaus at K₀ = 0.0245 for ε ≲ ε_c ≈ 0.22 before joining the ε² law — the near-degenerate system supplies its own detuning.

**What Γ_st is, and is not.** It is the initial slope of P_I(t), and we now name it a *short-time escape rate*. A shape diagnostic of P_I(t) shows a first transient followed by a plateau, with late-to-early slope ratios of 1/50 to 1/100 (often of negative sign): at s = 0.5, extrapolating 1/Γ_st = 1.8×10⁵ predicts P(2×10⁴) = 0.82 against **0.994 measured**. The loss saturates. Earlier drafts quoted lifetimes; that language did not survive the diagnostic and is withdrawn. The plateau is reported alongside as a second observable (§5(b)(iv)) and not as a claim, since it is *not* box-independent — it grows by ×1.5–4 from N = 48 to N = 64.

Γ_st(s) is strictly monotone, and truncation-stable for s ≳ 1.2 only (§5(b)(i)); the entries at s ≤ 0.9 are quoted as indicative. Below the classical shore, 1/Γ_st spans 10⁵ → 5·10² units of system time. **Above** the shore — inside the truncation-stable window — the quantum escape is *slower* than the classical blow-up (1/Γ_st vs t_blow = 311/54, 256/25, 213/52, 191/14 for s = 1.3–1.6, i.e. ×4–14): the quantum system disrespects the shore in both directions — it leaks below and lags above. Notably, the quantum escape time is smooth in s where the classical blow-up times fluctuate through the riddled boundary — the ħ-blur averages over the fractal shore.

**(b) Reliability of the leak.** (i) Truncation convergence: Γ₆₄/Γ₇₂ = 0.89–1.09 across the range in this implementation (N = 44 visibly underestimates — wall too close — as it should). **This is the one place where the reimplementation contradicts the bundle, and we flag it rather than arbitrate it.** With its own pre-registered criterion and its own fit window, the separate codebase reproduces the ratio above mid-island (1.10 at s = 1.2; 0.95 at s = 1.6) but obtains 2.0 at s = 0.9 (borderline: its pre-registered Γ₄₈/Γ₇₂ = 1.39 stays in band) and 6.2 at s = 0.5 (clear failure), with the plateau drifting the same way — i.e. the signal moves, not just the estimator. Protocol or physics is undecided. We therefore claim the rate table above mid-island and quote it as indicative below; the *existence* of the leak at small amplitude does not rest on it, but on the no-ghost control (§5(a′): ×10⁷ at s = 0.5) and on the fixed-shell observable. (ii) The flux is shell-independent: moving the detection edge n_c = 26 → 42 changes Γ by ×1.5 total (×1.11 per 4 levels); an evanescent tail would collapse by orders of magnitude. (iii) Regularization robustness: replacing the hard truncation wall by a soft taper of the quartic beyond n ≈ 48 (turning the outside into a free lead) changes Γ by factors 0.78–0.91 only; the *outgoing* rate is a property of the island state, not of the box. The eternal fate of the self-adjoint extension is explicitly outside our claim. (iv) Long-time behaviour (t = 2·10⁴, free by exact diagonalization): rise then noisy plateau with no deep returns; the plateau grows with box size at moderate/high s (0.101 vs 0.045 at s = 1.2 for N = 72 vs 44).

**(c) The leak is structural, not a preparation artifact.** Amputating the coherent state's tail at the classical shore (hard cut n ≤ 8: zero support beyond the island at t = 0) changes Γ by **1%** — the dynamics regenerates the flux from pure island support. Squeezing in *either* direction increases Γ (×1.4 to ×5.6): the coherent state is a near-minimal leaker; "thinner tails ⇒ slower leak" is false. Pure Fock states leak at comparable rates (|2,1⟩: ×3.3; |7,3⟩ at the shore: ×31): no classical coherence required. Spectral census *(reimplementation)*, re-measured on the fixed shell of (a′) so that the observable is truncation-invariant by construction: among the 51 eigenstates with island weight > 0.5 (island = {n₁, n₂ ≤ 8}, N = 72), the shell weight has median 4.2×10⁻³ and minimum 1.9×10⁻⁸, and **not one state approaches binding** — there are **no bound island eigenstates**: the island region of Hilbert space admits no invariant subspace, and every island state is a resonance. The criterion is relative, not absolute, which is what the positive control buys:

The region column is explicit because the two observables are not the same set, and we refuse to compare across definitions silently. Rows 2–4 are the like-for-like comparison: same N, same region, same code — in the bounded twin every island-dominant eigenstate is bound to machine precision, in the ghost system none is, with a median 10¹³ higher. Row 1 is the truncation-invariant headline; since the fixed shell is *contained* in the beyond-edge region, the twin's weights bound its own shell weights from above, so the twin is bound a fortiori on row 1's observable as well. The instrument detects binding when binding exists, and the ghost floor is therefore not a numerical floor — the earlier absolute threshold (10⁻⁹) is superseded by this comparison, which does not degrade as extreme-value statistics wander. Robustness: the minimum varies by ×2, non-monotonically, over N = 48/56/64/72 (1.2 / 1.4 / 2.5 / 1.9 ×10⁻⁸), and the state realising it changes with N — a floor shared by several deep states, not a fragile outlier; tightening the island definition from n ≤ 8 to n ≤ 6 drops the count from 51 to 32 and leaves the minimum unchanged to all digits; and a *rational* ratio ω₂ = 2, which thins the free spectrum by ×21 (190 distinct levels out of 4096 at N = 64), gives 1.5×10⁻⁷ — so the census reports the geometry of the island, not the arithmetic of the free spectrum. (Earlier drafts quoted 9.7×10⁻⁶ over 67 states with a beyond-edge region that grows with the box; same verdict, different observable. The fixed-shell version is the one we stand behind.)

**(d) The ħ-dependence is a steep power law, not (yet) an instanton.** Along the chain (g, s) = (0.05, 0.70) → (0.025, 0.99) → (0.0125, 1.40) — identical classical point g·s² = 0.0245, effective ħ halved then quartered — the rate falls 7.7e−5 → 4.8e−6 → 1.8e−7: factors ×16 then ×27. A pure e^(−S/ħ) law demands ×16 then ×256. In this deep-quantum window (island actions of order 1–2ħ) the suppression is Γ ~ ħ⁴⁻⁵; the mechanism (high-order perturbative flux through the resonance web vs pre-asymptotic tunnelling) is undecided, and the semiclassical regime is out of reach at our sizes (initial-tail contamination at the edge; N ≈ 96 would be required). Corollary: at fixed g, the growth of Γ(s) is dominated by classical proximity to the shore, not by ħ. This does not contradict GSTZ's exponential-suppression picture for the ground state at small λ — but it bounds it: the two regimes must be joined by a crossover that neither work has located. We regard this as a sharp open question produced by the confrontation.

**(e) Follow-up: the form of Γ_st(s, g), under a pre-registered protocol.** Extending to s = 0.15–0.45 with a guard battery (anchors reproduced first, two observables, window halves, box invariance N = 64/72, oscillation residual, pre-registered predictions with mechanical exclusion): all three candidate global laws for Γ(s) are excluded (at s = 0.30: e^(−b/s²) by ×1100, e^(−b/s) by ×55, s^p by ×8.3). Instead, Γ(s→0) approaches a **floor** Γ₀ ≈ 1.4×10⁻⁶ (1/Γ₀ ~ 7×10⁵), which persists for the pure Fock ground state, is regenerated by the dynamics (hard cut at n ≤ 4 changes it by 5%), and carries an avowed ×2 systematic (box invariance marginal, 0.58, for |0,0⟩). We read Γ₀ as the width of the ground-adjacent resonance cluster — the state computed in [4] §3.1, now with a number. The floor's coupling dependence is **non-perturbative**: Γ₀ = 1.6×10⁻⁶ → 1.0×10⁻⁹ → ~10⁻¹⁵ for g = 0.05/0.025/0.0125 (the last at the numerical representation floor, single truncation) — g² and g⁴ are excluded by 2–6 orders, while the pre-registered e^(−c/g) extrapolation (c ≈ 0.37) lands within 27% of the far-edge observable across nine orders of magnitude. Together with (d), the two regimes of the crossover question are now measured separately: non-perturbative suppression for the ground cluster (the tunnelling-like picture of [4], quantified) versus the soft ħ⁴⁻⁵ resonance-web law for amplitude-carrying island states. The excess Γ(s) − Γ₀ at fixed g grows ~s³ at small s, steepening toward the shore. (This subsection postdates the two full human re-runs and awaits the same treatment. It also lives entirely in the small-s regime where the reimplementation finds the rate not truncation-converged, §5(b)(i): the floor's *existence* is separately supported by the no-ghost control and by the fixed-shell observable, but its numerical value carries that reserve on top of the ×2 systematic already avowed.)

## 6. Honest map to prior work

**6.1 Quantum.**
- **Smilga [1,2]:** we do not contradict the conjecture — we give it its first controlled numbers in the generic case, and extend it to island states, where the −κ/r² heuristic (low-lying, singularity-supported states) was silent. His 2009 open point on the Bender–Mannheim realization with interactions is addressed here in the Hermitian-indefinite realization.
- **Damour–Smilga [3]:** untouched — their conjecture is scoped to globally benign systems; ours is the island case they explicitly left open. Our result blocks any *local* extension of the conjecture: local classical benignity does not imply quantum benignity.
- **GSTZ [4]:** the concept and the classical framework are theirs. We supply the rates (none existed), the island-state program (they treat only the ground-like state), the map of the physical interaction they set aside (with the qualitative 1:1 difference), and the ħ power law that bounds the tunnelling analogy in the deep-quantum island regime.
- **Deffayet, Fathe Jalali, Held, Mukohyama, Vikman [5]:** exact complementarity. Their theorem: global protection → discrete spectrum. Our measurement: insular protection → metastability, at rates given above. Together: *the topology of the protection decides the quantum fate of the ghost.* We also note that the PU-induced quartic sits exactly at the **marginal degree** of the dominance criterion of Deffayet–Held–Mukohyama–Vikman (JCAP 11 (2023) 031): expanding (q₁+q₂)⁴ gives self-interactions and ghostly interactions of equal degree, ℓ = m+n = 4 — consistent with the observed island-only (rather than global) protection, and perhaps a useful boundary case for that criterion.
- Adjacent works we have checked at abstract/intro level and believe distinct: Ilhan–Kovner [6] (one special unitary interacting example); Salvio [7] (metastability at the quadratic-gravity level); Errasti Díez–Gaset Rifà–Staudt [8] (classical taxonomy and global-stability method; islands as definition L1); Holdom [9]. We have **not yet** fully checked Boulanger et al. [10] and Kaparulin–Lyakhovich–Nosyrev [11] against our classical laws, and flag this openly.

**6.2 Classical maps and inter-degree structure (dedicated prior-art review, 2026-08).** A review of the 2004–2026 literature (arXiv hep-th/gr-qc/math-ph/nlin.SI/quant-ph, journals, proceedings, theses, Russian and English) assessed the classical claims — **novelty only, not correctness**. Verdicts:
- **s\*(p, ω₂) cartography** (dense grids, K = g·s^{p−2} as single parameter): *no prior art found* for the map itself; the g-rescaling invariance is elementary and not claimed. Qualitative overlap only: the existence of a threshold ("stable below a critical value", Pavšič [15]; the island "shrinks to a point" at equal frequencies, Damour–Smilga [3]).
- **Threshold-vs-detuning power law** (s\* ∝ Δ^β, β ≈ 0.9–1 at p = 4): *no prior art found*; Damour–Smilga's shrinking island is consistent with β > 0 but unquantified. (Register status: β at p = 5, 7 is **unmeasured** — two rounds returned non-concluding, by power and by construction; the claim is scoped to p = 4.)
- **Resonance canyons at rational ratios + parity/rank selection rule**: *no prior art found* for threshold dips at rational ω₂/ω₁ in an autonomous ghost system. The closest structural precedent — Avendaño-Camacho, Vallejo, Vorobiev [12], where the reduced-space singularity type changes with the ratio — proves *stability* in all their cases, quartic only, small coupling: no threshold, no canyon. Their invariant-ring machinery (two actions plus a resonant pair with a Hopf-type relation) is, conversely, the natural frame in which the §4.2 rule could be promoted from verified selection rule to theorem — a stated aim, not a result. Kaparulin–Lyakhovich–Nosyrev [11] use "resonance" for the degenerate 1:1 root, and construct stable interactions rather than measure unstable thresholds.
- **Zero-parameter Chirikov predictor** (pendulum width of the (4,2) channel, self-dressed ratio, closed loop): the criterion itself is classical (Chirikov; Lichtenberg–Lieberman; Escande [16]) and is claimed here **as a methodological transfer only** — its use as a zero-parameter predictor of a blow-up threshold edge in an autonomous, indefinite-energy system has *no published analogue found*.
- **Inter-degree affine law and its annihilator E(ω₂)** (refutation 11/11, CANYON-E at 2.52): *entirely novel* — no publication relates thresholds across polynomial degrees or tests an inter-degree invariant. Adjacent only: ODE blow-up theory (Souplet; Balabane [17]) for the C^{1/(p−2)} behaviour.
- **Degree as abscissa vs degree as condition**: recent analytic work (Held [13]; Deffayet et al. [14]) uses the polynomial degree as a *sufficient condition* for stability — a binary criterion — never as the variable of a measured threshold law. The two uses are complementary, not overlapping. In field theory, high polynomial degree *stabilizes* through dispersive decay; mechanics has no dispersion — so the s\*(p, ω₂) map is the dispersion-free skeleton of the problem, the worst case that field-theoretic dressing improves upon.

Review caveats, carried as-is: two primary sources remain to be checked figure-by-figure (Kaparulin–Lyakhovich proceedings 2015; Boulanger, Buisseret, Dierick, White [10]); the April 2026 cluster (arXiv:2604.21348, 2604.21823, 2604.21826) is recent and may be revised; and the review evaluated the claims' novelty, **not their correctness** — correctness rests on the artifacts of §2.3 and on external scrutiny, which is what this note solicits.

## 7. Limitations and open items

**Quartic bundle, classical:** T = 400, one initial-condition family, grid resolution ±1 cell; C empirical. **Quantum:** one main system (1, √2), one main g, occupations 1–15 (deep quantum, ħ = 1); "escape" operationalized as flux across n = 34 in a truncated basis — the continuum limit is an extrapolation, and the eternal dynamics of the self-adjoint extension is outside our claims (only the outgoing rate is claimed); the rate is a *short-time* rate — island survival plateaus after the initial transient, so no asymptotic lifetime is claimed — and it is truncation-stable only for s ≳ 1.2, the entries below mid-island and the floor of §5(e) being indicative; the plateau itself is not box-independent; the census verdict is robust but the shell weights at the bottom of the island are not converged in magnitude (§5(a′)); the form of Γ(s) is pinned by a follow-up run (§5(e)): no single law — a floor plus a resonant excess; the Γ(s) fit windows for s ≥ 1.3 required the island-survival estimator (edge-flux windows catch the arrival front); the mechanism behind the ħ⁴⁻⁵ law is undecided; the near-degenerate quantum case is untouched (quartic stiffness ~1/Δ²). Under the successor protocol, three quantum rounds were attempted and returned **zero verdicts**; the quantum branch is paused pending a redesigned estimator (register rule), for which four derivation templates have been identified in the recent literature (spectral type as a stability-class diagnostic [2,5]; operator-conservation moment bounds, arXiv:2604.21348; unitarity/vacuum criteria, arXiv:2604.21823; the interaction-induced drift of the Kaparulin–Lyakhovich bounded integral [18]), and none of the quantum numbers above have yet been re-derived under that protocol — a stated asymmetry between §4 and §5.

**Inter-degree campaign, open items (frozen order of attack):** the site trilemma at 8/3 — step back in geometry, make the killer zone itself measurable via the interleaved-set sieve, or revise the site prediction — decision pending, with the E landscape derived on the window as input; the shape of the E-well at 5:2 (no reading); |δ₅|/|δ₇| unmeasured (the core died); mirror points at 2:1; the derivation of C ≈ 1/4 (staged: structural triage, then coefficient extraction; frozen discriminant: rational C vs C containing π); the full (A₁, A₂) plane; a theory of the interleaved sub-threshold set — sharpened by M15: the set is degree-selective and site-adjacent at two independent sites. Also queued: the blow-up-profile check (one-parameter fit of the §4.1 ansatz on archived trajectories, settling finite-time blow-up vs asymptotic runaway in the taxonomy of [8]); a mechanism candidate for the measured 3:1 saturation — the reduced phase space at 3:1 is cuspidal rather than conical [12], and the channel-width construction may collapse there, which would turn the measurement into a derivation; and a negative-control round of the full pipeline on a system with *proven* stability (the integrable class of [12] or a bounded potential [15]), frozen expectation: no threshold up to a declared ceiling.
